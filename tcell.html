How to enter and attack a rooted tree

Entering the tree

Each vertex is represented by a single letter. The root must be A. Enter each edge by typing the letters at its two endpoints together.

For example, AB, AC, BD, BE describes the edges A–B, A–C, B–D, and B–E. You may separate edges with commas, spaces, semicolons, or line breaks.

When you choose Verify and Draw Tree, the page checks that A is present, that every vertex is connected to A, that no edge is repeated, and that the graph contains no cycle.

How one attack works

  1. Choose uniformly at random one edge whose two endpoints are still present.
  2. Choose uniformly at random one endpoint of that edge.
  3. Eliminate that endpoint and all edges incident with it.
  4. Check whether the root A still has a path to at least one leaf of the original rooted tree.
  5. If such a path remains, repeat the process. If not, the tree is broken.

During Attack Once, each successive diagram keeps the original layout. Previously eliminated material is gray, while the most recently selected edge and most recently eliminated vertex are specially highlighted.

Estimated number of attacks

Estimate Expected Number of Attacks runs 10,000 independent attacks. It reports the mean number of eliminated vertices required to break the tree. For each n, the table also reports the percentage of trials in which the tree had broken by the time n vertices had been eliminated.
