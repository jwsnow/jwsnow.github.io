Original observational research data

OASIS Student Data Tables

These tables come from the Open Access Series of Imaging Studies (OASIS) at Washington University in St. Louis. OASIS-1 contains cross-sectional MRI measurements and participant characteristics; OASIS-2 follows participants across multiple visits. The values here were extracted directly from the supplied oasis_cross-sectional.csv and oasis_longitudinal.csv files, not simulated.

Participants were volunteers selected for MRI research, not a random sample of the general population. The older research participants were drawn from studies associated with the Washington University Alzheimer Disease Research Center; the datasets were assembled to include participants with and without dementia.

How the records were selected

The age-60 restriction is applied only to the OASIS-1 tables titled Dementia vs. Sex, Dementia vs. Level of Education, Dementia vs. Socioeconomic Status, Brain Volume vs. Dementia, Dementia vs. College Attendance, and Brain Volume vs. Dementia Severity. Each contains one scan per participant, drawn from the 198 people aged 60 or older. This keeps the dementia comparisons within an older age group, since many younger OASIS-1 participants were not assessed for CDR.

No age cutoff was imposed for Intracranial Volume vs. Sex or Age vs. Brain Volume. Those tables contain the earliest scan from each of the 416 OASIS-1 participants, ages 18–96.

OASIS-2 already enrolls older adults: first-visit ages range from 60 to 96. The education table has one first-visit record per participant. The conversion table uses repeated observations from the 13 eligible converted participants. No additional age restriction was imposed on either OASIS-2 table.

In tables with a dementia classification, CDR = 0 means no dementia and CDR > 0 means a dementia classification. One person contributes at most one observation to any table except the explicitly paired before-and-after table.

Representativeness: These are research volunteers recruited for brain-imaging studies, not a random population sample. The proportion classified with dementia here is not an estimate of population prevalence.
